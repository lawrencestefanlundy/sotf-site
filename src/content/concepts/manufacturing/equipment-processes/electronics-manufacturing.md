## Physics / mechanism

Electronics manufacturing, in the sense captured by the available sources, is the industrial chain that turns bare substrate into functioning assemblies: printed circuit board (PCB) fabrication, followed by printed circuit board assembly (PCBA) in which components are populated onto the finished board. The two stages are tracked as separate markets, with PCBA the larger of the pair by revenue.

The key economic parameters visible in the sources are market scale, growth rate and geographic concentration rather than process physics. The global PCB market was roughly $89-90bn in 2025 growing at about 5.2% CAGR, while capacity share is heavily skewed: Asia holds over 70% of global PCB capacity, with China alone accounting for more than 60% of global production capacity.

Regional output is concentrated in a small number of firms and countries. Europe accounts for roughly $9.07bn, about 12.2% of global revenue, clustered in Austria, Germany and Switzerland, where AT&S, Schweizer and KSG together represent more than half of EU output. US fabrication output fell from more than 30% of the global total in the early 2000s to under 5% today.

## Competitive landscape

The sources support only a geographic comparison, not a technology comparison. On that axis, the landscape is a single dominant bloc (Asia, and specifically China) facing two much smaller incumbent bases: a European cluster with roughly an eighth of global revenue concentrated in three countries and three firms, and a US base that has contracted to marginal share. Any reshoring thesis therefore starts from a position where Western fabrication capacity is a small fraction of demand.
