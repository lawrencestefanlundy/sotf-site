## Physics / mechanism

Encapsulation covers a family of processes in which an active material is fully enclosed by a second phase chosen for its inertness, transparency or dielectric properties. In two-dimensional device stacks the encapsulant is itself a crystal: hexagonal boron nitride (hBN) or a transition metal dichalcogenide is transferred above and below the active layer so that the sensitive material never contacts a rough oxide or ambient air. The function is partly mechanical and chemical (flat interface, no adsorbates, protection against strain and dielectric disorder) and partly electronic, because the encapsulant sets the dielectric screening environment and can inject proximity effects across the van der Waals gap. For monolayer semiconductor excitons, encapsulation in atomically flat hBN is treated as a prerequisite for high optical quality, which is what makes integration with plasmonic nanoparticle lattices difficult, since the encapsulant separates the exciton from the confined plasmonic field optimizing strong light matter coupling of plasmonic lattice. Screening from hBN encapsulation is also large enough that atomistic Bethe-Salpeter calculations must include it to reproduce the observed spectrum of moiré intralayer excitons in WS2/WSe2 heterobilayers origin of trapped intralayer wannier and charge transfer exc.

When the encapsulant is chosen for its band structure rather than its inertness, it becomes an active functional layer. Encapsulating monolayer graphene between two parallel twisted WSe2 monolayers produces pure valley-Zeeman spin-orbit coupling, detected as a symmetry-enforced reordering of Landau levels driven by competition between the fixed valley-Zeeman term and the applied field tailoring pure valley zeeman spin orbit coupling in wse2 enc. The same architecture changes thermal transport: a WSe2 encapsulation suppresses out-of-plane energy dissipation from graphene hot carriers, raising the cooling length to about 2.68 um and lowering the heat-exchange coefficient, which decouples responsivity from bandwidth in zero-bias photodetectors c band 160 gbs 1 zero bias graphene photodetectors breaking.

A second branch is endohedral encapsulation, where the host is a hollow nanotube and the guest sits inside the cavity. Filling single-wall carbon nanotubes with charge-transfer molecules such as the donor tetrathiafulvalene or the acceptor tetracyanoquinodimethane provides doping that is shielded from the environment while leaving the outer nanotube surface free for later processing steps; filling routes include melt filling and solution-phase methods, and they differ in yield and stability comparative evaluation of encapsulation methods for endohedr. Boron nitride nanotubes serve the same geometric role with a wide-bandgap, optically transparent wall that limits direct electronic participation of the host, and have been filled from the liquid phase with quaterthiophene, sexithiophene, octithiophene and Nile Red liquid phase encapsulation of pi conjugated dyes in boron ni.

Encapsulation also appears as a barrier layer grown in situ and as a soft-matter container. Levitated ~200 nm Au nanoparticles in a quadrupole ion trap develop an evaporation barrier when repeatedly pulsed with 532 nm laser light at 1e-8 to 1e-7 Torr, tracked through precision charge-to-mass ratio measurements; the barrier grows in mass over prolonged vacuum exposure and, when formed under CO, is rapidly removed by subsequent O2 exposure carbon encapsulation of levitated au nanoparticles. In the biological case, encapsulating protein samples in lipid-coated water-in-oil droplets replaces surface passivation of sample chambers, with pegylated lipids preventing protein accumulation at the water-oil interface, enabling fluorescence correlation spectroscopy on nanomolar samples in nanolitre volumes biorxiv lipid coated water in oil droplets as a passiv.
