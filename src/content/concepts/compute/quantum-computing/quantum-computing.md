**Quantum computing encodes information in coherent quantum states to run algorithms with no efficient classical equivalent; the hardware exists and is on commercial clouds, but physical error rates of 10^-3 to 10^-4 sit several orders of magnitude above the sub-10^-9 rates practical applications need, so the whole field now turns on error-correction overhead rather than qubit counts.**

## Summary

A quantum computer stores information in two-level (or multi-level) quantum systems and manipulates it with unitary gates, exploiting superposition and entanglement so that certain computations scale better than any known classical method. The physical substrates differ sharply: superconducting circuits (IBM's Heron and Nighthawk generations are used as testbeds in recent work), trapped ions in modular Quantum Charge-Coupled Device architectures, semiconductor spin qubits shuttled between quantum dots, photonics with cryogenic integrated modulators, and trapped-ion qudits that use more than two levels per particle. None of these is yet coherent enough to run a useful algorithm directly.

On the algorithm side the honest position is that broadly useful speedups remain unproven. A recent review of Decoded Quantum Interferometry states that establishing a clear and scalable advantage on broadly useful practical optimisation problems remains challenging, while noting strong evidence of a superpolynomial speedup for approximate solutions to one specific structured instance, the optimal polynomial intersection problem. In chemistry, resource analyses concede that frequently considered applications require substantial resources and that the gap between hardware capabilities and requirements remains significant.

## Viability (3/5)

**TLDR: Hardware and small logical qubits are real; the error-rate gap to useful computation is three to six orders of magnitude and the path across it is mapped but not walked.**

## Drivers (3/5)

Demand: the sources do not support an assessment of market pull. There are no revenue figures, procurement commitments, funding totals or customer counts anywhere in this evidence base. The only demand-shaped signals are indirect: multitenancy is pursued because it increases throughput and reduces costs on cloud quantum machines, which implies contention for scarce capacity, and the cryptographic threat model assumes scalable quantum hardware may threaten classical cryptography within the coming decade, a driver for defensive spending rather than for quantum computing itself. The score of 3 rests on supply-side breadth alone and should be read as provisional.

**TLDR: Supply side is broad and industrialising across at least five modalities; the sources say almost nothing about paying demand.**

## Novelty (3/5)

The comparison that matters is against the best classical method for the same task, and the field's own reviews are careful here. For Decoded Quantum Interferometry, strong evidence of superpolynomial speedup over the best classical methods exists for approximate solutions to the optimal polynomial intersection problem, a special instance of the max-LINSAT class, while a clear and scalable advantage on broadly useful practical optimisation problems remains challenging. That is a real theoretical result and a narrow one. In quantum chemistry the framing is similar: nanographene pi-systems are proposed precisely because they are scalable problems that span the gap between early and large-scale fault tolerance, an admission that headline applications are out of reach. Notably, the same work finds Trotter error estimates varying by orders of magnitude depending on whether worst-case, average-case or eigenvalue errors are used, which means published resource comparisons are sensitive to methodology.

There is also a novelty question about which physics you should be using at all. The thermodynamic computing programme argues that stochastic classical hardware can natively run generative diffusion, Bayesian inference and Monte Carlo sampling, with the explicit analogy that thermodynamic chips emulate Langevin's equations the way quantum computers emulate Schrödinger's <sup class="ref"><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06584" title="Thermodynamic AI and the Fluctuation Frontier" rel="noopener">ref</a></sup>. For sampling-flavoured workloads that is a competing, room-temperature route. Quantum computing's defensible novelty is narrower than the general-purpose framing suggests: structured algebraic optimisation, and simulation of quantum systems themselves. A 3, contested, is the supported score. Benchmarking hygiene is a further caution, since evaluating distributed quantum compilers on random circuits inflates cut costs, alters scaling trends and reorders strategy rankings relative to real algorithmic circuits.

**TLDR: Superpolynomial advantage is credible only on narrow structured problems; against the general classical baseline the case is still open.**

## Diffusion (2/5)

Access is not the bottleneck. Superconducting processors are reachable as multitenant cloud resources, a trapped-ion qudit processor was available for protocol demonstration, and software-engineering practice is being imported, including mutation testing for quantum neural networks. The barriers are capability and trust. Capability: fewer than 60 qubits on leading trapped-ion machines and error rates five to six orders of magnitude short of application requirements. Trust: sharing a device between tenants creates inter-circuit crosstalk that is structurally predictable enough to be a channel, with Grover circuits behaving as universally aggressive interferers at t-statistics of 1.37 to 2.61 against standalone baselines and quantum Fourier transform circuits disproportionately susceptible. Any commercial multi-user service must solve that before enterprise adoption.

Secondary barriers are ecosystem-level. Quantum benchmark suites are small enough that researchers substitute random circuits, which then misleads compiler design. Decoders that work offline do not necessarily meet latency budgets in hardware, which is the explicit motivation for redesigning belief propagation plus ordered statistics decoding. Scaling by networking modules imports the whole quantum communications problem set, from repeater-grade entanglement sources to satellite links still contending with atmospheric loss, pointing and tracking, payload constraints and synchronisation. Diffusion in the sense of researchers using machines is happening now; diffusion in the sense of production workloads is not visible in these sources.

**TLDR: Cloud access and toolchains exist, but the machines cannot yet do work anyone needs, and shared use introduces its own security problems.**

## Impact (4/5)

The value case is asymmetric rather than broad. Simulation of molecules and materials is described as a promising application of fault-tolerant machines, with nanographene pi-systems offered as chemically relevant and scalable targets. Structured optimisation gains a superpolynomial advantage on at least one problem family via Decoded Quantum Interferometry. The clearest quantified consequence is destructive: scalable quantum hardware is widely anticipated to threaten classical cryptography and the current information security infrastructure within the coming decade, because Grover and Shor become operationally realisable, which is already driving redesign of systems such as blockchains that depend on classical cryptographic assumptions. A technology that forces the re-engineering of global cryptographic infrastructure is high-impact whether or not its positive applications land.

A 5 is not earned by these sources. They contain no demonstrated commercial value, no economic sizing, and repeated concessions that the headline applications remain out of resource reach, plus an explicit statement that broadly useful advantage is unestablished. The impact score is therefore a judgement about conditional magnitude, not about realised value, and it should be read alongside the viability and timing calls.

**TLDR: If fault tolerance arrives, the affected areas are molecular and materials simulation, structured optimisation and public-key cryptography.**

## Timing Later (5-10yr)

The one time-anchored expectation in the evidence base is defensive: the cryptographic threat is anticipated within the coming decade. Chemistry work explicitly targets the interval between early and large-scale fault tolerance, which is consistent with early fault-tolerant demonstrations in the next few years and useful computation later in the decade. Five to ten years is the band the sources support; anything sooner would require an error-rate improvement these papers do not report.

**TLDR: Component-level fault tolerance is being demonstrated now; useful fault-tolerant computation is gated on error-rate and overhead reductions that these sources place several architecture generations away.**

## Overrated or underrated? Fairly rated

The position worth taking is that the interesting variable is no longer qubit count but overhead per useful logical operation, and that investors should read announcements accordingly. Two specific discounts apply. First, resource estimates are methodology-sensitive, with Trotter error estimates spanning orders of magnitude depending on which error measure is used. Second, compiler and partitioner benchmarks built on random circuits distort cost, scaling and even the ranking of strategies relative to real algorithmic workloads, so software-layer claims should be checked against real circuits before being believed. Anyone selling near-term optimisation advantage on current hardware is ahead of the evidence in these sources.

## Prediction

By 1 May 2029, no peer-reviewed or preprint result will report a quantum machine achieving a scalable, practically useful advantage on an unstructured optimisation problem outside structured algebraic classes such as optimal polynomial intersection.
