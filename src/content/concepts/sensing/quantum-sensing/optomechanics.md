## Physics / mechanism

Optomechanics describes the coupling between optical fields and mechanical motion, typically mediated by radiation pressure or by the dependence of an optical resonance frequency on a mechanical displacement (dispersive coupling). In a cavity geometry, a mechanical mode of frequency Ω_m shifts the resonance of an optical cavity of linewidth κ, while the intracavity field exerts back-action on the mechanics. The single-photon coupling rate g₀ sets the interaction strength; a fibre-based Fabry-Perot microcavity coupled to a suspended hexagonal boron nitride drum in a membrane-in-the-middle geometry has reached g₀/2π ≈ 180 kHz with strong radiation-pressure back-action unresolved sideband optomechanics with hexagonal boron nitri.

The ratio κ/Ω_m defines two operating regimes. Optomechanically induced transparency is usually treated in the resolved-sideband limit, but compact microcavities often sit in the unresolved-sideband limit (κ ≫ Ω_m), where varying pump power and cavity detuning drives a crossover from a transparency-like dip to a gain feature in the reflected response, reproducible with the full linearised model unresolved sideband optomechanics with hexagonal boron nitri. Multimode systems allow light to couple otherwise non-interacting mechanical resonators, producing non-Hermitian behaviour including exceptional points at which eigenfrequencies and eigenvectors coalesce, a prerequisite for chiral mode dynamics and topological state transfer exceptional points in diamond optomechanics. Waveguide implementations based on backward stimulated Brillouin-Mandelstam scattering reach the strong-coupling regime, identified by normal-mode splitting and avoided crossings, in tapered chalcogenide photonic crystal fibre at relatively low pump power and room temperature quantum limits to ground state cooling of traveling hypersou.

For sensing, the levitated branch removes clamping losses: optically trapped dielectric objects offer extreme environmental decoupling for force, acceleration, torque and rotation measurement optimal displacement detection of arbitrarily shaped levitat. Geometry matters: rod- and dumbbell-shaped particles have been used for torque and rotation sensing, while high aspect ratio plate-like particles can exhibit reduced photon recoil heating and are candidates for high-frequency gravitational wave detection or high-bandwidth accelerometry optimal displacement detection of arbitrarily shaped levitat. ## Competitive landscape

The field splits by how the mechanical element is held and how the light is confined. Clamped-resonator platforms (photonic-crystal nanobeams, diamond optomechanical crystals, membrane-in-the-middle microcavities) give lithographic control over coupling and connectivity; diamond additionally supports strain coupling to spin defects for hybrid quantum technologies exceptional points in diamond optomechanics, and engineered nanobeam circuits use transverse geometric asymmetry in the photonic-crystal cavity to break the cancellation that suppresses dispersive coupling to in-plane flexural modes, making megahertz flexural modes optically bright coupled flexural optomechanical cavities with engineered nan. Levitated platforms trade fabrication control for extreme isolation and are the preferred route for force, torque and acceleration sensing optimal displacement detection of arbitrarily shaped levitat. Waveguide Brillouin optomechanics operates on travelling hypersound phonons rather than localised modes and can reach strong coupling at room temperature quantum limits to ground state cooling of traveling hypersou.

A third axis is whether optical and mechanical confinement are co-designed or decoupled. In flame-produced surface nanoscale axial photonic resonators, the frequency of optically excited mechanical modes is independent of the details of optical axial confinement and matches the fundamental breathing mode of an infinite solid cylinder, indicating geometrical uncoupling of the optical and mechanical degrees of freedom cavity optomechanics in an infinite cylinder.
