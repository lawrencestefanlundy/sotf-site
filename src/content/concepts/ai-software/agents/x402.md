The HTTP 402 status code, reserved since the earliest HTTP drafts and never standardised, turned into a live payment negotiation between an HTTP client and server, settled in stablecoins, with no accounts, no API keys and no human in the loop.

> Researched 21 July 2026 (x402 protocol state). Read the volume section carefully before quoting any number from it.

## Mechanism

Four steps. The client requests a protected resource. The server returns **402** with a `PAYMENT-REQUIRED` manifest listing scheme, network (CAIP-2 id), asset, amount, destination, timeout. The client signs a payment payload and retries with a `PAYMENT-SIGNATURE` header. The server verifies, settles, and returns 200 with a `PAYMENT-RESPONSE` receipt. V2 renamed the V1 `X-PAYMENT` headers and is backward compatible.

**Payment schemes** are the pluggable part:

| Scheme | Semantics | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `exact` | Pay a specific amount. Gasless on EVM via EIP-3009 `transferWithAuthorization`, Permit2 as ERC-20 fallback | Fixed-price calls |
| `upto` | Client authorises a maximum, seller settles what was consumed | Metered inference and compute, where cost is unknown at request time |
| `batch-settlement` | Buyer funds an on-chain escrow once, then signs an **off-chain voucher** with a cumulative amount per request; seller redeems many payments in one transaction | The sub-cent economics unlock. Spec status **11 May 2026** |

**Fees: zero at the protocol layer, and there is no native token.** You pay gas. Facilitators may charge, and several advertise free.

Microsoft appears in the April initial-member list and is absent from the July roster. Check the live page before putting Microsoft in anything.

**Acknowledged gaps in the protocol itself:** no chargebacks, no dispute resolution, no agent trust-scoring. Refunds and escrow are on the roadmap, unbuilt. Card networks and stablecoin rails have incompatible reversibility and liability regimes, and x402 joins them technically without harmonising them legally.

## Volume, and a measurement problem that has to be stated

Two incompatible series are in circulation and the gap is roughly **20x**.

**Protocol-reported (x402.org's own panel, the figure the press now quotes):** 75.41M transactions and $24.24M over the 30 days to 21 Jul 2026, ~29 tx/sec, ~$0.32 average. CoinDesk quoted the same panel on 15 Jul and noted the Linux Foundation announcement itself carried no usage figures.

**Independent on-chain trackers:** 157.8M cumulative settlements and $41.1M cumulative volume across 7 chains and 18 facilitators as at 21 Jul 2026, with **monthly settlement increments flat at ~4-5M all through 2026**.

These cannot both be measuring the same thing. 75M payments in 30 days exceeds the entire cumulative on-chain settlement increment for that month, and $24.24M in 30 days is more than half the cumulative $41M ever settled on-chain. The likely reconciliation is that x402.org counts protocol-level payment events including off-chain batch-settlement vouchers, which by construction produce one on-chain settlement per thousands of HTTP payments, and the headline inflects immediately after batch settlement shipped on 11 May 2026. No source reconciles them. **If you use one number, use both and say which is which. Do not quote 75M transactions and $24M as on-chain volume.**

**The earlier series holds up.** The peak of **$5.15M (Nov 2025)** collapsing about **77% to $1.19M by May 2026** is verified. The "~$1.6M/30d wash-filtered" figure carried on **Agentic Payment Rails** could not be reproduced from any source; x402scan reads $1.11M and Major Matters $1.2M for the same window. That figure should be dropped.

**Wash share.** Artemis in February 2026 put roughly half of observed transactions at self-dealing or wash trading, with daily volume then around $28,000. No July 2026 split exists anywhere, and claims that it has fallen are qualitative. Chainalysis is the constructive counterweight: the $1+ payment band rose from 49% to 95% of value between early 2025 and early 2026, the $0.10-$1 band fell from 46% to 4%, and tester-to-payer conversion improved fourfold over six months. Retention hit 87% in October 2025 on PING memecoin speculation and crashed to 5% when the speculators left.

**Has the curve turned up?** Mix-shift rather than demand. On-chain settlements are flat. Reported dollar volume roughly doubled in June 2026, concentrated overwhelmingly in a single seller, BlockRun, an AI inference router. Chain share is moving off Base toward Solana and Polygon.

Scale check: $24M per month against Visa's $14.2 trillion in FY2025.

## Ecosystem

**Edge infrastructure is the July 2026 story.** AWS CloudFront and WAF shipped x402 monetisation as **generally available**, with publishers adding a "Monetize" action inside Bot Control rules, enforced before origin. Amazon Bedrock AgentCore Payments went to preview in May 2026, built with Coinbase and Stripe, exposing Coinbase's x402 Bazaar MCP server with 10,000+ endpoints. Cloudflare's Monetization Gateway opened a waitlist on 1 July 2026, still waitlist-only, with payment rules written like WAF expressions across 330+ points of presence. Context for why Cloudflare cares: 52% of crawler requests were for AI training by June 2026, up from 22% in spring 2025.

**Both the AWS and Cloudflare implementations currently route through Coinbase's facilitator** for verification and compliance screening. That is the most investable fact on this page.

**Sellers** are concentrated in machine-readable data and search APIs, LLM inference (largest per payment), web automation and trading analytics. BlockRun dominates by volume.

## Adjacent protocols

Mostly stacked layers rather than substitutes, with one real collision.

| Protocol | Owner | Layer | Relation to x402 |
|---|---|---|---|
| AP2 | Google, donated to FIDO Apr 2026 | Authorisation, proves a human authorised the agent. Does not move money | Complementary. See [Payment Mandates](/ai-software/agents/payment-mandates/) |
| ACP | OpenAI + Stripe | Checkout inside an AI surface | Adjacent; competes with UCP |
| UCP | Google + Shopify, Jan 2026 | Merchant journey | **UCP vs ACP is the one real head-to-head** |
| Visa Intelligent Commerce / Trusted Agent Protocol | Visa | Tokenised card credentials | Competing rail, but Visa is a premier x402 member |
| Mastercard Agent Pay / AP4M | Mastercard | Sub-cent programmatic payments on Mastercard's network | Direct competitor to x402's core use, and Mastercard is also a premier member |
| L402 / Lightning | Lightning Labs lineage | HTTP 402 over Bitcoin | The intellectual predecessor, effectively lapped |

x402 has won the standards fight decisively and has not won the volume fight in any absolute sense. It has won a market that barely exists. Cards win human-authorised retail checkout; x402 wins machine-to-machine API and inference metering at sub-dollar sizes, because cards cannot economically clear $0.05.

## The investment read

Open protocol, Linux Foundation governance, zero fee, no token. There is nothing to own at the protocol layer, so value has to accrue adjacently, and the candidates are:

- **Settlement chains.** Sequencer fees on every settlement. At Base's ~$0.0001/tx, 75M monthly payments is $7,500 of fees, so the unit economics are hostile even at 1000x volume.
- **Stablecoin issuers.** Reserve yield on ecosystem float, requiring no protocol-specific view. See [Stablecoins](/ai-software/agents/stablecoins/), and note that issuance economics are deteriorating for unrelated reasons.
- **Facilitators.** Commoditising toward zero margin on verification, with several free public facilitators. But the margin was never in verification. It is in compliance, sanctions screening, and being the regulated entity two of the world's largest edge networks will sign a contract with. That is Coinbase's today, at ~58.7% cumulative share.
- **Identity, trust and clearing.** The biggest hole, and the reason [Agent Identity (KYA)](/ai-software/agents/agent-identity-kya/) keeps getting named as the opportunity. Read that page before acting on it, because the hole is not the same thing as a business.
- **Discovery and routing.** Bazaar (Coinbase, 10,000+ endpoints). Arguably the real bottleneck, and the layer Coinbase is furthest ahead on.
- **Application-layer sellers.** BlockRun's dominance suggests the profit sits with whoever owns demand rather than the pipe.

The pattern across all six is that Coinbase monetises facilitation, compliance, discovery and the settlement chain at once, and is the counterparty of record for both AWS and Cloudflare. The listed card networks are a hedge rather than a competitor bet, since both sit on the board.

The weak point in that read is that demand for machine-to-machine micropayments could stay flat for another two or three years, in which case these positions are early rather than wrong, and early is hard to tell from wrong on a fund clock.
