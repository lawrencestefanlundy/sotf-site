The general-purpose bipedal (or human-form upper-body-on-mobile-base) robot, bet on because the human-built world is shaped for the human form, so one platform could in principle do many jobs without re-tooling the environment. The form factor that captured the 2024-26 capital wave.

## State (mid-2026)

12+ commercial humanoid models exist (up from ~3 in 2024). Real deployments are narrow, structured-environment, and largely teleop-assisted or fine-tuned demos; genuine autonomous general-purpose work is not here. The brain is a [VLA](/robotics/robot-learning/vision-language-action-models/) (usually dual-system: a slow VLM planner + a fast ~200Hz visuomotor policy), the hard part is [dexterity](/robotics/embodiment/dexterous-manipulation/) and [touch](/robotics/embodiment/tactile-sensing/), and the body is gated by [actuators](/robotics/embodiment/robot-actuators/) (Optimus paused production late-2025 on hand/arm/motor issues).

## Players

- **Europe:** **Neura Robotics** (4NE-1, ~€98k, up to $1.4bn Series C, the European champion).
- **US:** Figure ($39bn, Helix VLA, BMW pilot), 1X (NEO, consumer, TaaS), Apptronik (Apollo, ~$5.3bn), Agility (Digit, most real warehouse deployments), Tesla Optimus (production paused late-2025), Boston Dynamics (electric Atlas, Hyundai-owned).
- **China:** Unitree (aggressive vertical integration + low price), plus a fast-commoditising mid-tier.

## Economics (the bear note)

Humanoid ASP fell from ~$85k (2023) to ~$25k (2025); Unitree runs ~60% gross margin via the Chinese supply chain. This is a PC/EV-style race to the bottom forming at the OEM layer, which is why the investment read routes value to components and data, not OEMs. The market-structure bet is **Humanoid Robot Market**; the autonomy-timing bet is **Robot Autonomy Destination**.
